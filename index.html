DOWNHILL

SANTA CRUZ V10
WHAT IS DOWNHILL?

Built to go down. Fast.

Downhill mountain bikes are designed around one main priority: descending steep, technical terrain with as much control, stability and confidence as possible. Almost every part of a DH bike is designed with descending performance in mind. They normally use long-travel suspension, aggressive geometry, powerful brakes, strong wheels and frames designed to cope with hard impacts and rough terrain.

They are ideal for riders who spend their time in bike parks, on lift-accessed trails, downhill tracks or racing. If your favourite part of mountain biking is attacking steep sections, rocky terrain, roots, jumps, drops and high-speed descents, a downhill bike is specifically designed for that environment.

The biggest compromise is climbing. DH bikes are generally heavy and their geometry and suspension are designed for descending rather than efficient uphill pedalling. That means they aren't normally the best choice if you want one bike for long everyday rides or trails where you need to climb for every descent.

✓ IDEAL FOR

  • Bike parks
  • Lift-accessed riding
  • Downhill racing
  • Steep technical descents
  • Big jumps and drops
  • Rough, high-speed terrain
  • Shuttle days
  • Maximum descending control

✕ NOT IDEAL FOR

  • Long climbs
  • Everyday trail riding
  • XC riding
  • Long-distance rides
  • Efficient uphill pedalling
  • One-bike-does-everything use

THE AGGRESSIVE ALL-ROUNDER
WHAT IS ENDURO?

Climb it.
Send it.

Enduro bikes are designed to give riders a strong combination of pedalling ability and serious descending performance. Compared with a downhill bike, an enduro bike is much more practical when you need to ride uphill under your own power, but it still has enough travel, geometry and component strength to handle aggressive descents.

They are particularly well suited to mountain trips and places such as the Alps, where a single ride can involve a long climb followed by a steep, technical descent. Enduro bikes can also be excellent for bike parks, technical local trails and riders who enjoy pushing their bike hard downhill without wanting to give up the ability to pedal back to the top.

The trade-off is that an enduro bike isn't usually as efficient uphill as a lighter trail bike, and it isn't as specialised for pure downhill racing as a DH bike. Its strength is versatility: it gives you a bike capable of handling a huge variety of serious mountain terrain.

✓ IDEAL FOR

  • Technical climbs and descents
  • Alps and mountain trips
  • Enduro trails
  • Bike parks
  • Steep and rough terrain
  • Long mountain rides
  • Aggressive trail riding
  • Riders wanting versatility

✕ NOT IDEAL FOR

  • Pure XC racing
  • Maximum climbing efficiency
  • Very smooth easy trails
  • Long-distance road-style riding
  • Pure downhill racing
  • Riders wanting an ultra-light bike

TRAIL

THE EVERYDAY MTB
WHAT IS TRAIL?

One bike.
Loads of possibilities.

Trail bikes are designed to be versatile. They are made for riders who want to climb efficiently, descend confidently and spend an entire day exploring different types of terrain without having a bike that is overly specialised for one discipline.

A good trail bike can be an excellent choice for everyday mountain biking, local forests, technical singletrack, longer rides and trips where you don't know exactly what terrain you will encounter. They generally offer a more balanced combination of weight, suspension, pedalling efficiency and descending ability than more specialised enduro or downhill bikes.

The trade-off comes when the terrain becomes extremely aggressive. A trail bike can absolutely handle technical riding, but if your main goal is repeated huge jumps, serious downhill racing or extremely rough bike-park tracks, an enduro or DH bike may be better suited.

✓ IDEAL FOR

  • Everyday trail riding
  • Local forests and trails
  • Climbing and descending
  • Longer adventures
  • Exploring new trails
  • All-round mountain biking
  • Riders wanting versatility
  • One-bike setups

✕ NOT IDEAL FOR

  • Serious downhill racing
  • Repeated huge jumps and drops
  • Extreme DH tracks
  • Maximum bike-park performance
  • Riders who only descend
